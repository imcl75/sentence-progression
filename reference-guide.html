Colour-Coded Sentence Progression

A Teacher's Reference Guide — Year 1 to Year 6

Wallscourt Farm Academy edition. The colour-coded sentence-progression framework and its Year 1–6 structure sequence are the work of Jacobs Education (Primary English Consultancy). This guide restates that framework in a cleaner, typeset layout for staff reference, alongside the school's companion interactive tool.

Overview

Reference Guide

This guide sets out a colour-coded approach to teaching sentence construction across Years 1–6, following the sentence-type progression set by the National Curriculum. Each part of a sentence — its subject, its verb, its object, and so on — is given a consistent colour and symbol, so that the same visual language carries through every year group, only growing more complex as pupils do.

The parts of a sentence used throughout this guide are: who (the subject — who or what the sentence is about), doing (the verb), what (the object), where, when, how (an adverb), describe (an adjective), an unlabelled diamond for auxiliary and modal verbs (is/was/has/might…, where the verb isn't itself the action), and three connecting parts: co-ordinating and subordinating conjunctions, and relative clauses.

A note on "who": the subject of a sentence isn't always a person — "the ball" is the subject of "The ball deflates." From Year 3 onwards, this guide labels the block who (subject) to make that explicit as pupils' grammatical vocabulary develops; Years 1–2 keep the simpler term "who" to match the concrete, person-icon-friendly language young children need.
This guide can be used flexibly: follow a class's own year-group table for whole-class teaching, or drop down to an earlier year's table to scaffold a pupil working below age-related expectations, or up to stretch one working ahead.
